Course description

From the college catalog: This course provides students with the theory and skills needed to secure networks using virtual private networks (VPNs) and firewalls. Various network security-related issues, including risks, threats, and vulnerabilities are introduced and examined. The focus is on best practices and strategies for network security and incident response. Different types of VPNs for securing data in an organization are discussed as well as the benefits and architecture of a VPN and how to implement a VPN. Other topics include the utility and the limitations of a firewall. Instruction is also given on the functionality of a firewall, how to construct, configure, and administer a firewall, secure network design, and layered network security strategies.

This section teaches those skills on Palo Alto Networks next-generation firewalls, following the Palo Alto Networks Firewall Essentials (EDU-210) curriculum. You build a firewall configuration from initial setup through security zones, Security and NAT policy, application and user identification, threat prevention, URL filtering, malware analysis, and decryption, then use logs and reports to investigate what the firewall sees. The course closes with cloud-delivered security, SASE and CASB, including how VPN-based remote access is giving way to Zero Trust Network Access.

How the course works

The course is fully online and asynchronous; there are no live sessions. Each week has a due date on the schedule. This 3-credit course is delivered in 11 weeks instead of the usual 15, so plan on about 12 hours per week: roughly half reading, videos, and simulations, and half in the lab.

Deadlines and late work

  • Your role. Plan your time well. Technical issues, deadline confusion, and schedule conflicts are not excuses for lateness. NDG labs can take longer than expected, so don't start them the night they're due.

Getting help: 3 Before Me

Before asking your instructor about a problem, try at least three different resources: course materials (this site's readings, simulations, lab companions, and glossary), online resources such as Palo Alto Networks TechDocs, and your classmates. If you're still stuck, reach out and share what you found in those three places. It makes the answer faster and the learning stick.

AI use

This is a skills-based networking and cybersecurity course, and AI tools such as ChatGPT can support your learning when used appropriately.

Acceptable uses

  • Asking for explanations or summaries of course topics.
  • Getting hints or guidance on how to approach an assignment, not completed answers.
  • Troubleshooting and debugging advice while working on technical tasks.

Unacceptable uses

  • Copying and pasting AI responses directly into assignments. This is treated as plagiarism.
  • Using AI during quizzes, exams, or any other assessment meant to be completed without outside help.

You are responsible for understanding and following this policy; if a use seems unclear, ask first. Violations may result in a reduced assignment grade, a failing course grade, or further disciplinary action by the college. The design challenges in Modules 15 and 16 unlock a model answer only after you write your own; if one is assigned in Canvas, submit your own words.

Certification

This course follows the Palo Alto Networks EDU-210 Firewall Essentials curriculum. The older PCNSA and PCNSE exams have been retired. The closest current Palo Alto Networks credential for this material is the Network Security Generalist certification; the Next-Generation Firewall Engineer certification is a further step. "Exam tip" boxes in each module flag ideas that certification exams test often.

Academic integrity

Academic integrity is submitting your own work and properly acknowledging the work of others. Any violation of this principle is academic dishonesty, including:

  • Plagiarism: submitting another's work, in whole or part, as your own. This includes an examination, a computer program, a laboratory report, or a written assignment. Copying AI-generated responses into assignments is plagiarism (see AI use).
  • Facilitating academic dishonesty: helping another commit an act of dishonesty, such as substituting for an examination or completing an assignment for someone else.
  • Cheating: using or attempting to use unauthorized materials on an examination or assignment, such as unauthorized texts or notes, or improperly obtaining or attempting to obtain copies of an examination or its answers.
  • Illegal system access: altering, transmitting, or permitting unauthorized individuals access to your account, or attempting to alter or destroy system files on any server or computer, including academic information or records.

Students who participate in dishonest activities will receive a 0 for that project, examination, or assignment, may be given a grade of F for the course, and may be reported to the Dean for disciplinary action. For more information, see the current Brookdale Community College Student Handbook.
