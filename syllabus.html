Course description

This course teaches students to deploy, configure, and manage a next-generation firewall at the network perimeter. Students build a firewall configuration from initial setup through security zones, security and NAT policy, application and user identification, threat prevention, URL filtering, malware analysis, and decryption, then use logs and reports to find what matters. The course closes with cloud-delivered security: SASE and CASB. Hands-on labs run on Palo Alto Networks firewalls in NDG Online.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, you will be able to:

  1. Describe next-generation firewall architecture, including single-pass processing and separate control and data planes.
  2. Perform initial firewall configuration and manage licenses, content updates, and software.
  3. Manage candidate and running configurations and administrator accounts using local and external authentication.
  4. Segment a network with security zones and configure tap, virtual wire, and Layer 3 interfaces with routing.
  5. Write, order, test, and troubleshoot Security and NAT policy rules.
  6. Build application- and user-based policy with App-ID and User-ID.
  7. Block known and unknown threats with Security Profiles, URL filtering, and WildFire.
  8. Configure SSL/TLS and SSH decryption and handle decryption exceptions.
  9. Use the dashboard, ACC, logs, and reports to investigate traffic and threats, and forward logs to external systems.
  10. Explain SASE and CASB architectures and how they extend perimeter security to remote users and SaaS.

Certification

This course follows the Palo Alto Networks EDU-210 Firewall Essentials curriculum. The older PCNSA and PCNSE exams have been retired. The closest current Palo Alto Networks credential for this material is the Network Security Generalist certification; the Next-Generation Firewall Engineer certification is a further step. "Exam tip" boxes in each module flag ideas that certification exams test often.

Deadlines and late work

  • Deadlines. Assignments (quizzes, labs, and other coursework) are due one week from the assigned date. Exact due dates and times are in Canvas; the schedule on this site shows the weekly dates.
  • Late penalties. Late submissions lose 10% per week (about 1.43% per day). After four weeks, late work is not accepted and earns a zero.
  • Emergencies. Contact your instructor about possible exceptions in an emergency, with the required documentation.
  • Your role. Plan your time well. Technical issues, deadline confusion, and schedule conflicts are not excuses for lateness. NDG labs can take longer than expected, so don't start them the night they're due.

Getting help: 3 Before Me

Before asking your instructor about a problem, try at least three different resources: course materials (this site's readings, simulations, lab companions, and glossary), online resources such as Palo Alto Networks TechDocs, and your classmates. If you're still stuck, reach out and share what you found in those three places. It makes the answer faster and the learning stick.

AI use

This is a skills-based networking and cybersecurity course, and AI tools such as ChatGPT can support your learning when used appropriately.

Acceptable uses

  • Asking for explanations or summaries of course topics.
  • Getting hints or guidance on how to approach an assignment, not completed answers.
  • Troubleshooting and debugging advice while working on technical tasks.

Unacceptable uses

  • Copying and pasting AI responses directly into assignments. This is treated as plagiarism.
  • Using AI during quizzes, exams, or any other assessment meant to be completed without outside help.

You are responsible for understanding and following this policy; if a use seems unclear, ask first. Violations may result in a reduced assignment grade, a failing course grade, or further disciplinary action by the college. The design challenges in Modules 15 and 16 unlock a model answer only after you write your own; if one is assigned in Canvas, submit your own words.
