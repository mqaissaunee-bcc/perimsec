Cloud Access Security Broker (CASB)

Your organization's most sensitive data probably lives in someone else's application: a file-sharing service, a CRM, a collaboration suite. And employees use hundreds of SaaS apps nobody approved. A CASB finds them, decides which are allowed, and protects the data inside the ones that are. This module covers CASB in general, then Palo Alto Networks SaaS Security.

    Part A

    CASB in general

    What SaaS changes, and the two ways a CASB sees it.

    16.1

    The SaaS problem

    Data you're responsible for, in systems you don't run.

    In SaaS, the provider runs the infrastructure and application, but under the shared responsibility model the customer remains responsible for its data, its users, and how the service is configured. Several risks follow:

    • Shadow IT: employees sign up for apps with a work email and no review. A typical organization uses far more SaaS apps than IT knows about, and a firewall's App-ID view (Module 8) usually reveals the gap the first week.
    • Data exposure: files shared by public link, sensitive documents uploaded to personal accounts, or data copied from a sanctioned app into an unsanctioned one.
    • Misconfiguration: an admin setting such as "anyone with the link" sharing by default, or MFA not enforced, exposes everything in the app.
    • Compromised accounts and risky integrations: stolen credentials give attackers data without touching your network, and third-party apps granted OAuth access can read mailboxes or drives.
    • Malware in cloud storage: an infected file in a shared folder syncs to every user who has it.

    Apps fall into three practical groups: sanctioned (approved and managed by IT), tolerated (allowed for a business purpose but not managed, often with limits), and unsanctioned (blocked).

    16.2

    What a CASB does, and how it connects

    Four jobs, two vantage points.

    A Cloud Access Security Broker sits, logically, between users and cloud services. Its functions are commonly grouped into four:

    • Visibility: discover which apps are used, by whom, how much, and how risky each one is.
    • Data security: find and protect sensitive data, with data loss prevention (DLP) and control over sharing.
    • Threat protection: detect malware in SaaS storage, compromised accounts, and risky behavior.
    • Compliance: show auditors that regulated data is controlled and apps meet requirements.

    Inline and API

    Inline CASBAPI-based CASB
    Where it sitsIn the traffic path: an NGFW, a forward proxy, or a SASE serviceOut of band, connected to the sanctioned app's own API
    What it seesTraffic as it happens, to any app, sanctioned or notData at rest, sharing settings, users, and activity inside sanctioned apps, including what was there before you started
    Real-time blockingYes: block an upload or an app as it happensNo; it acts after the fact (remove a public link, quarantine a file)
    Unmanaged devices and outside collaboratorsOnly if their traffic passes through itYes, because it sees the app's data regardless of who connected
    Typical useDiscover shadow IT, block unsanctioned apps, control actions like uploadsScan sanctioned apps for exposed sensitive data and malware

    The two are complementary, which is why most CASBs offer both. (Some also use a reverse proxy in front of a sanctioned app for unmanaged devices.)

    16.3

    Posture management and app triage

    Configure the sanctioned apps safely, and decide about the rest.

    SaaS Security Posture Management

    SSPM connects to sanctioned apps and checks their settings against best practices and compliance frameworks: is MFA enforced, is external sharing restricted, how many people have admin rights, which third-party apps have been granted access? It reports misconfigurations and can often fix them. It's the SaaS equivalent of reviewing a firewall's configuration for bad rules.

    Triage

    For each discovered app, weigh its risk (the vendor's security practices, certifications, breach history, data handling terms, account options such as SSO and MFA) against its business value (how many people use it, for what, and whether a sanctioned app already does the job). Then tag it sanctioned, tolerated, or unsanctioned and apply controls to match.

    16.4

    Palo Alto Networks SaaS Security

    An integrated CASB that uses the firewall and Prisma Access as its inline path.

    Palo Alto Networks SaaS Security combines several components:

    ComponentModeWhat it does
    SaaS Security InlineInlineIntegrates with NGFWs and Prisma Access to discover SaaS apps, score their risk from dozens of attributes, and turn sanctioning decisions into policy.
    SaaS Security APIAPIConnects to sanctioned apps to scan files and sharing for exposures, external collaborators, sensitive data, and malware.
    SSPMAPIChecks sanctioned apps' settings and third-party integrations.
    Enterprise DLPInline and APIConsistent data patterns and policies across the firewall, Prisma Access, and SaaS apps.

    In Strata Cloud Manager the inline discovery views are labeled Discovered Apps and the API scanning is labeled Data Security; older documentation uses the names in the table. Licenses are sold as bundles, so which components an organization has depends on what it bought.

    How it builds on this course

    • App-ID (Module 8) is the inline discovery engine: every SaaS app has an App-ID, and many have separate App-IDs for actions such as upload, post, or share.
    • Tags and application filters (Module 8): tagging apps sanctioned, tolerated, or unsanctioned lets one Security rule block every unsanctioned app through an application filter, and new apps can be handled as they're discovered.
    • HTTP header insertion (Module 11) restricts users to the organization's own tenant of apps such as Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, blocking personal accounts on the same service.
    • Data Filtering and File Blocking (Module 10) are the inline data controls; Enterprise DLP extends them.
    • Decryption (Module 13) makes it work: nearly all SaaS traffic is encrypted, and action-level control requires seeing inside it.

    Exam tip

    "Block personal accounts on a sanctioned SaaS service while allowing the corporate tenant" points to HTTP header insertion. "Block an entire category of unsanctioned apps, including future ones" points to an application filter on tags.

    16.5

    Putting a SaaS policy together

    Discover, decide, control, protect, review.

    1. Discover: use inline visibility (App-ID, SaaS Security Inline) to see which apps are used and by whom.
    2. Decide: triage each app as sanctioned, tolerated, or unsanctioned, with the business owners.
    3. Control: block unsanctioned apps by tag; allow tolerated apps with action-level limits such as no uploads; restrict sanctioned apps to the corporate tenant.
    4. Protect: connect sanctioned apps by API to scan data and sharing, and apply DLP inline and by API.
    5. Review: monitor posture with SSPM, watch logs and reports (Module 14), and revisit tags as apps and needs change.

    The inline half of this list runs on Security policy, App-ID, profiles, and decryption, exactly the skills from this course, whether the enforcement point is the firewall in the lab or a security processing node in Prisma Access.

    Summary and self-check

    • SaaS shifts infrastructure to providers, but customers stay responsible for data, users, and configuration. Risks include shadow IT, data exposure, misconfiguration, compromised accounts, risky integrations, and malware in storage.
    • A CASB provides visibility, data security, threat protection, and compliance.
    • Inline CASB sees live traffic to any app and can block in real time; API CASB sees data at rest and sharing in sanctioned apps, including from unmanaged devices. SSPM checks sanctioned apps' configuration.
    • Apps are triaged as sanctioned, tolerated, or unsanctioned, each with matching controls.
    • Palo Alto Networks SaaS Security combines inline discovery and control with NGFWs and Prisma Access, API scanning, SSPM, and Enterprise DLP, building on App-ID, tags and filters, header insertion, Data Filtering, and decryption.

    Key terms

    CASB, shadow IT, SSPM, sanctioned app, data loss prevention.
