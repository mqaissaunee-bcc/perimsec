Perimeter Security

Learn to configure, manage, and monitor a next-generation firewall, from the first login on the management port to decrypting traffic and reading the threat log. Sixteen modules over eleven weeks, each with readings, simulations, a lab companion, and a self-check quiz.

How a week works

Read the week's modules here and run every simulation; they are part of the reading. Then do the lab in NDG Online and take the graded quiz in Canvas. Each module's self-check quiz tells you when you are ready.

Labs

Labs run on a live firewall in NDG Online. Every module has a lab companion that tells you what the lab is for, what to check before you start, and the mistakes that cost students the most time.

Your work stays with you

Enrichment

Putting these skills in employer language

The work you did in this course shows up in real job postings, usually in slightly different words. Postings often say “next-generation firewall” or list several vendors at once (Palo Alto Networks, Fortinet, Cisco, Check Point). The ideas you practiced carry over: zones, rule order, NAT, application-aware policy, decryption, and log analysis work the same way on other platforms, even when the menus don’t.

On a resume, pair the phrase with what you actually did and where, for example “configured source and destination NAT and security policy on a Palo Alto Networks firewall in a lab environment.” Hiring managers trust specifics, and you can explain every one of these in an interview. Modules 15 and 16 also give you a fair claim to “familiarity with SASE, ZTNA, and CASB concepts.”

This course follows the Palo Alto Networks EDU-210 Firewall Essentials curriculum, which lines up with the company’s role-based certifications, starting with the Palo Alto Networks Certified Network Security Professional (formerly Network Security Generalist). Earning it gives employers independent evidence for every row above.
